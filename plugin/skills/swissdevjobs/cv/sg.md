# CV conventions — Singapore (`sg`)

Applies to: mycareersfuture.

## Format

- **1–2 pages.** "Resume" and "CV" are used interchangeably; nobody will
  mind either word. Two pages is comfortable for anyone past their first
  role.
- **No photo.** Older Singaporean templates included one and you will still
  see them, but the fair-employment guidance below has pushed the market
  away from it. Omit unless a posting explicitly asks.
- **Never put your NRIC or FIN on a CV.** Personal-data guidance restricts
  when organisations may collect national identifiers at all, and a full
  NRIC on a document you email to strangers is a real identity-theft
  exposure. If an application form demands one, that is the form's problem,
  not something to pre-empt on the CV.
- A short professional summary (2–3 lines) at the top is conventional.

## Work-pass status — the thing that differs most from Europe

This is the single biggest divergence from the other countries in this
tool, and getting it wrong wastes everyone's time.

Singapore has no "will you sponsor a visa?" question in the European
sense. Hiring a non-resident means the *employer* applies for a specific
pass, and which pass — and whether they can get it at all — depends on the
role, the salary, and the company's existing workforce mix. The practical
categories a posting cares about:

- **Citizen or Permanent Resident** — no pass required. Say so plainly;
  it materially changes the employer's cost and timeline.
- **Employment Pass (EP)** — professional/managerial/executive roles,
  subject to a minimum qualifying salary that rises with age and sector,
  and to a points-based eligibility assessment covering salary,
  qualifications, and the employer's workforce diversity and local hiring.
  Both the salary floor and the points framework are revised periodically
  — check the Ministry of Manpower's current rules rather than trusting a
  number you read anywhere, including here.
- **S Pass** — mid-skilled roles, additionally constrained by a company
  quota and a levy, which makes some employers unwilling regardless of fit.
- **Long-Term Visit Pass / LTVP+ holders** may work on a Letter of Consent.
  If this is you, say it — an LOC is far lighter for an employer than
  sponsoring a fresh pass, and it is a genuine advantage worth stating.
- **Dependant's Pass holders cannot.** The Letter of Consent route was
  withdrawn for DP holders on 1 May 2021; a DP holder who wants to work now
  needs their own Employment Pass, S Pass, or Work Permit, sponsored by the
  employer like any other candidate. Older advice online still says
  otherwise — it is out of date, and acting on it wastes an application.

**State your status in one line near the top.** "Singapore Citizen",
"Singapore PR", "Employment Pass holder (transferable)", or "Requires
Employment Pass sponsorship" — whichever is true. Burying it wastes a
screening round; overstating it collapses at the offer stage.

Note on why postings appear on MyCareersFuture at all: employers must
advertise most roles there for a minimum period before they may apply for
an Employment Pass, under the Fair Consideration Framework. So a listing on
this board does **not** by itself signal openness to foreign hires — some
of those adverts exist to satisfy the advertising requirement for a role
the employer intends to fill with a pass holder, and others are genuinely
local-only. Read the posting, and don't infer sponsorship willingness from
the venue.

## Content expectations

- **Notice period is expected.** One to three months is normal here and
  employers plan around it. State yours; it is not oversharing.
- **Current and expected salary** get asked far more directly than in the
  UK or US, often in the application form rather than the CV. It is
  reasonable to give a range, and reasonable to decline on the CV itself
  and discuss it in conversation.
- Achievements with numbers over duty lists, same as the anglo markets.
- Local context helps: recognisable Singapore or regional employers,
  and any SEA-market exposure, land well.

## Personal details to leave out

Fair-employment guidance discourages employers from asking about, and
candidates from volunteering, age, date of birth, marital status, family
plans, gender, race, religion, or nationality beyond what work-pass status
actually requires. Older local templates asked for all of these — the
conventions have moved, and a modern CV omits them. Work-pass status is the
narrow exception above, because it is a genuine operational fact about the
hire rather than a personal characteristic.

## Language rules

- English, always. Singapore's working language is English and postings are
  in English.
- British-leaning spelling is the local standard (organise, analyse), but
  American spelling is unremarkable and not worth a rewrite.
- Do not write in Singlish, and do not attempt local idiom you don't
  actually use.

## Cover letter

- Optional and increasingly skipped, like the UK. Write one when the
  posting or the ATS asks; keep it under 300 words, direct, tailored.
- "Dear Hiring Manager" is fine; a real name is better.
